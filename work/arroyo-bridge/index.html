The Arroyo Bridge

A 25-metre single-span pedestrian bridge, designed by architecture students and fabricated by robot. Its branching lattice, drawn from the vascular structure of leaves, was effectively unbuildable by conventional means, so it was welded from hundreds of individual steel members on a six-axis arm at the Autodesk Technology Center in Boston, then trucked across the country and installed over a canyon.

Role

Why a robot

Every node in the lattice meets at a different angle, so almost no two cuts or welds are the same. That's the case where a jig-and-fixture workflow collapses and a programmable arm starts to make sense. The setup cost is the same whether the next part is identical to the last one or not.

Getting it there

The bridge was built in sections in Boston and assembled on site in Los Angeles, which puts every accumulated fabrication error into a handful of field joints.
